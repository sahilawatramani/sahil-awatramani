# How the decisions are made

## Output contract and timing

`python checker.py PACK` writes `flags.csv` in the invocation directory. Its columns are exactly `station_id,channel,start_utc,end_utc,issue,action,note`. Intervals are inclusive, minute-formatted UTC. For absent data, boundaries are the first and last absent expected samples. For clock-affected records, flag boundaries use **corrected UTC**; the note identifies the source CSV line and correction. This avoids using an ambiguous original logger timestamp as the key to a repair.

`correct` means a reproducible mapping is justified, not that the input has been overwritten. The internal timeline is repaired for comparisons and coverage. `exclude` with a weather channel masks just that channel; `all` masks all seven. `metadata` exclusions restrict spatial interpretation, not all weather measurements. Overlapping masks are unions, so samples are not subtracted twice. A `keep` interval never overrides a more specific exclusion. Healthy intervals are otherwise implicit. Exact retransmissions are removed by source-row identity, not by an all-channel exclusion.

The CLI fails clearly on malformed timestamps or a missing required schema rather than silently losing unaddressable records. Missing or empty station files produce warnings and whole-station exclusion intervals inferred from the common network period and inventory cadence; absent trustworthy GPS produces a warning. An entirely empty pack is an error. Outputs are rendered into a temporary generation before replacement. Ordinary rename failures roll back replaced files; `manifest.json` is written last and lists SHA-256 hashes of all four outputs. A process or power failure between file renames is detectable by a missing or mismatching manifest, not magically atomic across filenames. Input hashes are checked before and after analysis, and a change aborts generation. The generation identity includes normalized executable source text and input hashes. There is no resampling or imputation.

## GPS and clock repair

NMEA payloads are separated from their logger timestamp at the first comma, and the XOR checksum between `$` and `*` must match. RMC accepts active `A` fixes and rejects invalid/estimated/manual/simulated modes. Coordinates use degrees plus minutes/60 with hemisphere signs and range checks. Fractional seconds and RMC date rollover are retained. GGA needs a real GNSS fix (quality 1, 2, 4 or 5), at least four satellites, finite HDOP in (0, 5], and altitude units M. Its altitude is mean-sea-level height; **GGA alone supplies no date** and is not used to infer one from the possibly wrong logger clock.

Logger-minus-RMC offsets are rounded to minutes to remove the pack's small receipt jitter. A clock regime needs at least three valid RMC fixes spanning two hours, each within three seconds of the rounded offset. Subminute drift is not repaired. Shifts larger than a day are withheld for review. A later regime is mapped to a unique original weather-row transition only when applying the change restores the inventory cadence within the bracket between old/new GPS regimes. The initial regime must have valid GPS evidence within two hours of the initial logger row. Unresolvable transitions withhold the station rather than risk deleting valid overlapping observations. Missing GPS does not prove bad weather, but limits time verification.

For S06, the repaired transition begins at corrected 2025-03-24 16:10, source CSV line 3411, with +21,600 seconds. It recovers 2,639 observations. The first valid shifted RMC is later than the transition; original row order localizes the break. Timestamps that repeat before repair represent distinct physical times, unlike S08's 144 identical retransmissions.

## Location, cadence and completeness

A location comparison requires at least 12 valid RMC fixes spanning a day. A mismatch exceeds both 1 km and ten times median GPS scatter. At least three independent good GGA positions agreeing within 100 m increase confidence. Neither source is blindly declared correct. S05's ~20.5 km discrepancy therefore restricts coordinate-dependent use, and it is omitted as a reference station in spatial checks.

Expected coverage uses the network's earliest/latest supported corrected observations, each station's inventory cadence and installation date. This assumes a common reporting period; a uniformly missing network prefix/suffix cannot be discovered. Missing whole files have nominal slots inferred from that period, with the inferred phase explicitly identified. Observed stations preserve their own cadence phase. Only timestamps on that grid count toward expected-slot coverage; extra off-grid rows cannot raise coverage above 100%. They remain available for review, with an irregular-cadence metadata flag. Records predating installation cause a metadata conflict; an empty eligible period displays N/A rather than a false percentage. Missing nominal slots are flagged in contiguous intervals. Proven retransmissions retain one first copy; unresolved clock data is never deduplicated. Unique out-of-order observations are sortable with source provenance preserved.

## Direct sensor checks

| Check | Evidence required | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Numeric / sanity | Non-finite or outside broad bounds: temp [-90,60] C; RH [0,100]%; station pressure [300,1100] hPa; wind [0,350] km/h; direction [0,360]; rain >=0; GHI [-5,1600] W/m2 | Exclude; never clip |
| Saturated humidity | At least 12 h continuously >=98%, with >=3 readings >100% or sunny target-minus-peer RH >25 percentage points | Exclude entire episode, including apparently plausible saturation |
| Frozen temperature, RH or pressure | Identical readings for >=12 h and substantial independent peer range (3 C, 15 RH points or 2 hPa); temperature may also use >=24 h persistence plus GHI range >200 | Exclude affected sensor only; do not apply to dry rain, zero nighttime GHI or calm winds |
| Temperature spike | More than 12 C from median of >=4 neighbors, within three cadences or 30 min, whose range is <5 C | Exclude isolated reversal; a sustained front is not this pattern |
| Temperature burst | Paired abrupt peer-residual edges >=12 C, duration <=6 h, clean 3-hour context each side, >=75% context coverage, return within 3 C and magnitude >6 robust noise scales | Exclude the bounded excursion; a coherent regional front cancels in the residual |
| Astronomical night GHI | >30 W/m2 for >=30 min with solar elevation <-6 degrees at interval midpoint, after clock repair | Exclude with medium confidence; twilight noise tolerated |
| Coherent mountain cold | Target >750 m above reference median, colder by >3 C and temperature correlation >0.8 across >=144 paired samples | Explicit keep, subject to other exclusions |

Bounds are broad screening heuristics for a weather network, not universal climatology or instrument specifications. Rain is interval accumulation; there is no cumulative-counter reset test. Direction 360 is accepted as north, and no arithmetic direction averaging is performed. Solar geometry follows NOAA's fractional-year approximation with east-positive longitude and UTC; it is a broad night check, not a precision radiation model.

## Peer evidence and uncertain diagnoses

For each target, select up to four stations within 180 km and 700 m elevation difference, ordered by distance plus elevation difference/50. Require at least two usable simultaneous peers. Locations under dispute cannot act as references. Broad elevation-aware selection and pairwise pressure baseline centering avoid treating normal elevation pressure differences as faults. Peers need not have identical local weather; no spatial-only outlier is enough for a numerical repair. Reference rows already rejected for time or direct sensor problems are omitted.

**Pressure step and recovery.** Center each target-peer pressure difference on its robust pack median (at least 144 pairs); the constant only removes site-level pressure differences. An adjacent residual jump >=2.5 hPa must create a >=4 hPa shift between 12-hour windows, exceed six robust noise scales (floor 0.15 hPa), and have >=75% of nominal paired observations in each window. Elapsed-time windows prevent outages from supplying stale records. Pairwise reference adjudication below must support the changed target. Suppress duplicate candidates within one window. A later confirmed return within max(1.5 hPa, 25% of the initial shift) of the pre-fault residual closes the exclusion at the preceding sample; it does not open a new pack-end exclusion. Changes without observed recovery remain open. The supplied S02 boundary stays March 21 17:20; this shorter-window estimator gives about +7.8 hPa, versus +6.8 in the earlier 48-hour estimator. Neither is a calibrated numerical correction.

**Wind changes on two time scales.** Use log((target+0.5)/(peer-median+0.5)), with nonnegative target and reference >=1 km/h. Hourly medians need >=30 minutes of observations. Short candidates need an adjacent hourly jump >=0.25 log units, a >=0.65 log-unit change across 12-hour windows, >=75% window coverage, and a shift >6 robust noise scales (floor 0.055). The preceding three days' hourly residual MAD must be <=0.15: a short-event diagnosis is unsafe when the normal target/reference wind relationship is highly variable. This requirement intentionally reduces sensitivity in complex terrain. The 0.5 stabilizer limits calm-wind instability; very calm periods can still prevent detection or recovery confirmation.

A longer check retains the earlier two-regime daily median model: reference wind >=5 km/h, >=336 eligible hours, >=21 eligible days (>=6 h/day), >=7 days per regime, ratio change >=factor 2, and absolute-loss reduction >=40%. Long candidates require independent 72-hour pairwise reference support. This check can miss short events, so it complements the short detector rather than replacing it. Candidate boundaries are refined against raw-cadence residuals; the apparent timestamp resolution is not proof of the physical onset. A return within 0.22 log units or 25% of the initial change closes the incident. Multiple short events are supported; the long model still fits one dominant split.

Repeat the fit while omitting each reference where at least two remain. Hold from the earliest nearby tested opening (within 12 h of the incident), retain the nominal opening and source rows, and do not extend an event using a distant, different dominant split. S07 survives 4/4 omissions; tested boundaries are March 26 16:00-19:50 UTC, and the precautionary hold starts at 16:00. The full-profile factor is about 0.27; it does not identify a factory conversion.

**Pairwise reference adjudication.** For each candidate, compare changes in every available pair among the target and up to four references, using the same physical-time windows. An edge means a stable pairwise difference: <1.5 hPa for pressure, <0.22 log units for short wind or <0.35 for the 72-hour wind check. Require >=75% paired coverage. Enumerate complete coherent groups in this small graph; chains are not quorums. Exclude a target only when every largest coherent group omits it and at least two references form a group. If equally supported groups contain/exclude the target, record an unresolved check, not a CSV `keep` or an invented calibration. A target in the largest stable group is not blamed. The explicit assumption is that the failed group is smaller than the stable reference group; common faults and faulty majorities can escape or defeat this reasoning. Geography/elevation is a suitability filter, not independent calibration truth.

**Daylight temperature drift.** Daily target-minus-peer median temperatures are split into GHI >300 and <5 W/m2, each needing >=2 h of data. Need >=28 days, first/last-week sunny shift >=2.5 C, night shift <=1.5 C, and day/residual correlation >=0.65. A robust nonnegative hinge trend is fitted across candidate onset days; slope must be >=0.06 C/day and absolute loss improve >=30%. Exclude individual contiguous GHI >100 windows from the full-model estimated onset, preserving nighttime values. Repeat the fit omitting each reference to expose sensitivity, without treating these runs as independent probability trials. S04 is detected in 3/4 omission checks, with successful fitted starts ranging March 12-April 2. The March 14 full-model boundary remains a cautious hold, not a known physical onset. Alternative weather, siting or peer artifacts remain possible. The model is evidence of a pattern, not a causal paint diagnosis. Direct-check stages, pressure, wind, and temperature fits use a shared prior mask snapshot; candidate evidence is collected before that stage applies exclusion decisions. Raw weather values remain unchanged, while the canonical timeline carries supported clock repairs. Incident evidence includes source rows, opening/recovery transitions and status.

**Separate time-block check for temperature.** Assume the first week is initially serviceable and fit median residual cells by two-hour UTC and 200 W/m2 sunlight bins, falling back to hour-only cells if sparse. Test the second week without refitting. Compare last-week sunny departures against that held-out period and nights separately. For S04, held-out sunny median absolute error is 0.775 C, last-week sunny departure +2.925 C, and last-week night bias -0.275 C. Three consecutive sunny days above a robust 3.113 C departure threshold first occur April 9. This independently supports later warming but does **not** validate the earlier March 14 hinge onset. The whole early sunlit hold remains a precaution that may withhold valid observations; the synthetic benchmark charges such early holds as false exclusions. No model establishes paint as the cause. Retained nights cannot stand in for daily means or maxima.

**Rain blockage/release.** A zero run >=48 h must be followed by >=5 mm within an hour and a pulse >=5 times the peer median. During the zero run, at least an hour's records must show two wet peers and target temperature >2 C; at least two peers must total >=5 mm. Exclude from the first corroborated missed wet interval through the nonzero release. This bounds evidence, not the physical start of blockage; the problem may begin earlier while dry. Do not distribute the pulse backward or estimate a complete storm total. Local intense rain without preceding missed peer events is retained by this check.

## Climate use and technician priority

Sample quality and aggregation eligibility are separate. Audit channels explicitly carry `full`, `repaired`, `partial`, `hold`, or `review` status, plus an aggregation restriction and a coordinate-dependent-use restriction. Unresolved reference changes require review of cross-boundary summaries without being asserted as individual sensor failures. Partial rain coverage does not permit a complete-period total; retaining S04 nights does not permit complete daily temperature means or maxima. High coverage is not high calibration accuracy.

Rank only problems active in the final day, excluding incidents with confirmed recovery. Compare feasible one-hour intervention classes first (configuration/connection, reference calibration, exposure diagnosis, remote metadata reconciliation), then reference-omission support and affected channel-hours in the last week. These are explicit ordinal operating assumptions, not a measured utility or repair probability. Rank each station once; already completed software repairs do not consume the field hour.

For this pack S07 outranks S02 because a documented model/units/logger mismatch may be fixable during the hour; S02 needs a calibrated station-pressure reference. Request sensor/configuration photographs before travel. The evidence notebook includes a timed procedure, required equipment, specification-based verification and a no-reference contingency. If wind is verified/fixed remotely, or the pressure reference is available while the wind specification is not, reconsider S02 first. S04 needs a sunny reference comparison and may require longer co-location; its diagnosis and onset are less certain. Ended S03/S05 incidents do not automatically outrank continuing failures.

## Limits and scaling

There is no trusted co-located ground truth. Common-mode faults can pass peer tests, and uncommon local weather can resemble a fault. S01 snow-related rain undercatch is plausible but unquantified, so there is no invented correction. No tests certify calibration, predict faults, reconstruct missing data, or turn partial-period statistics into a season. Multiple abrupt short incidents and their recoveries are modeled; the noisy-wind long check and daylight-drift fit still use one dominant split/onset. Faults below the thresholds, near pack edges, across outages or without sufficient references can be missed. Fixed thresholds are defensible screening choices, not tuned universal constants. Source schema changes need explicit parser updates. Organizer hidden-test and real field performance remain unknown. See BENCHMARKS.md for labeled synthetic evaluation, including small-shift misses, ambiguous groups and healthy-data loss; it is not field validation.

Spatial reference selections are cached during each run. At 500 stations, persist suitable preaggregated features across runs, version station history/thresholds, track input hashes, group incident windows and alert on current impact rather than every row. Validate using independently labeled examples and monitor alert volume and precision by season, terrain and station hardware. Require human signoff for geographic relocation, recalibration factors, rain redistribution and causal hardware diagnoses.
